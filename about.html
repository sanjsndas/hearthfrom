    About the journal

    Hearth · Form · Theory

    Independent editorial on interior design, architecture, materials and the built environment. We publish practical guides, critical essays and field notes for designers, architects and curious homeowners.

    Our story

    Design journalism with a point of view

    Hearth Form Theory began as a conversation between an architect and a writer who were tired of the same recycled interiors coverage. Too much of it was surface-level — pretty pictures without context, trends without consequence. We wanted something slower, more considered, and genuinely useful.

    Today, we publish in-depth articles, case studies and long-form field notes that explore how spaces actually work: how light moves through a room, how materials age, how a floor plan shapes the rhythm of daily life. We believe good design writing should be as thoughtful as good design itself.

    The most memorable rooms are often the ones that know what to leave out. We write about the spaces that get that balance right — and the thinking behind them.

    What we cover

    Our editorial spans interior design, residential architecture, materials, space planning, lighting, furniture and sustainable building. We're less interested in trends than in principles — the enduring ideas that make spaces feel right, decade after decade.

    Whether you're an architect specifying a facade, a designer planning a lighting scheme, or a homeowner about to renovate a kitchen, we aim to give you something you can actually use. That means clear explanations, real examples, and a healthy respect for both beauty and budget.

    Independence

    We are editorially independent. Our content is never paid for by brands or PR agencies, and we clearly label any sponsored or partner content.

    Depth

    We take the time to research, interview and think. Our articles are longer because the subjects deserve it — not because we like the sound of our own voice.

    Practicality

    Every piece aims to leave you with something actionable: a principle, a checklist, a question to ask your builder. Inspiration is good. Utility is better.

    Respect

    We write for a smart, curious audience. No jargon for its own sake, no dumbing down. We trust you to follow a good argument.

    Editorial principles

    We follow a simple set of rules. First: accuracy over speed. If we're not sure, we check. Second: transparency. We disclose our sources, our methods and our mistakes. Third: independence. We don't accept payment for coverage, and we don't let advertisers influence what we publish.

    We also believe that criticism has a place in design writing. Not everything new is good, and not everything old is worth keeping. Our job is to help you tell the difference.
